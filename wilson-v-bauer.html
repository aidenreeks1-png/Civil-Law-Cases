Wilson v Bauer Media

In 2017, actor Rebel Wilson sued magazine publisher Bauer Media over a series of articles about her identity and background. A civil jury had to decide whether the publications were defamatory and whether Bauer's defences succeeded.

Your challenge: Do more than guess who won. Identify which legal issue each piece of information relates to, evaluate the publisher's defences, and then tackle the harder question that later reached the Court of Appeal: how much loss was actually caused by the defamation?

Actual 2017 result

The jury found for Rebel Wilson

The jury rejected Bauer Media's defences. The trial then moved to a different question: damages.

The trial judge awarded Wilson a total of $4,567,472, including $650,000 in general damages and about $3.917 million in special damages for lost screen opportunities.

But the case was not over. Bauer appealed the damages award. Your next role is harder: you are now sitting as an appeal panel.

Court of Appeal challenge

Damage is not the same as causation

For this stage, accept that Wilson was defamed. The question is whether the evidence was strong enough to prove that the publications caused particular lost film opportunities and income.

Key distinction: Evidence that someone's reputation was harmed may support general damages. A much larger claim for specific financial loss requires a sufficient causal link between the wrongful publication and the claimed loss.

Actual appeal outcome: The Court of Appeal left the finding of defamation intact but set aside the special-damages award. Wilson's damages were reduced to $600,000. The problem was not whether the articles were defamatory; it was whether the evidence sufficiently proved that the publications caused the particular lost film opportunities claimed.
