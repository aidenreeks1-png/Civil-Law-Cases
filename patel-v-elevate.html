Case summary

Sixteen-year-old Maya Patel fell about three metres from the “Sky Run” obstacle at an indoor adventure centre in Sydney.

She suffered a fractured wrist, knee ligament damage, required surgery and physiotherapy, and missed ten weeks of casual work.

Maya says Elevate failed to take reasonable care. Elevate says its safety systems were reasonable and Maya ignored a clear instruction to stop if equipment felt unusual.

Maya must establish her civil claim.

Key learning:
A civil case asks whether a defendant is liable, usually on the balance of probabilities. A criminal jury instead decides whether the prosecution has proved the accused guilty beyond reasonable doubt.

How to Play

1. Read the evidence. Each file gives you a new piece of testimony, footage, a warning, or another record.
2. Analyse it. Decide whether that evidence supports Maya, Elevate, both sides, or neither. Then choose the strongest legal explanation.
3. Move the confidence meter. Before you lock an evidence item, show how convinced you currently are that Elevate is liable. Your view can change as more evidence arrives.
4. Lock your analysis. Once locked, you receive feedback and the case moves forward. Locked answers cannot be changed.
5. Go back whenever you need. Use the ← Back button to reread completed evidence or instructions. Review mode is read-only, so your score and progress are safe. Use Return to current step when ready.
6. Deliberate at the end. Choose liable or not liable, select your two strongest pieces of evidence, and write a short explanation using civil-law language.

Recommended: work in a group of four and discuss each decision before locking it.

Teacher Guide

Grouping: Best in groups of four with one device per “civil jury”.
Timing: About 20–30 minutes, depending on discussion.
Learning: plaintiff/defendant, liability, balance of probabilities, negligence, weighing evidence, civil jury role and contributory negligence.
Important: The final liable/not liable verdict is deliberately open. Either can be defended. The scoring rewards evidence classification and legal reasoning.
Debrief: Ask, “How was your role in this civil case different from a criminal jury?”
Accountability: Students record their decision code and one sentence explaining the verdict.
